# Tidewater demo guide

Use this app, not the older Tidewater deployment or its screenshots. Initial records and teams are synthetic, and each visitor has a private workspace. Use a clearly labeled synthetic observation for the recording, without faces or personal details. Do not present a task completion as real cleanup.

## Two-person walkthrough · approximately 3–4 minutes

1. **0:00–0:25 · Landing.** YOU: “This is Tidewater, our coastal reporting and task prototype. Coastal volunteer work depends on people, equipment, and transport. We wanted to make the next step after noticing a problem easier to organize.” Show the original shoreline illustration. Click **Explore the working demo**.
2. **0:25–0:55 · Overview.** FRIEND: “This is a private demo workspace, with six synthetic reports and three sample teams to get started. The numbers are demonstration data, not actual cleanup impact. Reports, tasks, and a map help us see what needs attention.” Point to the counts and map.
3. **0:55–1:40 · New report.** YOU: “I’ll add a synthetic observation. We give it a category, priority, description, and location. A map pin and coordinates make the location explicit. An optional photo is stored privately; the app removes image metadata, but does not automatically blur faces.” Click **New report**, fill the fields, optionally choose the generated test image, and click **Submit report**. Example title: “Demo litter beside the beach access.” Description: “Synthetic demonstration only: a small amount of litter beside a public path. No real incident is being reported.”
4. **1:40–2:35 · Task.** FRIEND: “Submitting creates a task. We can assign a sample team with a matching category, then claim it and record progress. The database checks each transition, so a task cannot jump straight from open to completed.” Select **Shoreline Stewards**, click **Claim this task**, then **Start task**. YOU: “Completion needs a note. For this demo we are only demonstrating the workflow.” Enter “Synthetic demo completion only. No actual cleanup was performed.” Click **Mark complete**, then close the dialog.
5. **2:35–3:15 · Map, teams, activity.** FRIEND: “The Coast map lets us open a report from its pin. Volunteer teams show their sample focus and equipment, while Activity records changes.” Navigate through **Coast map**, **Volunteer teams**, and **Activity**. Open a pin and team, closing each dialog before moving on.
6. **3:15–3:55 · Build and next steps.** YOU: “We wrote this new implementation during the event using React, TypeScript, Supabase, Leaflet, and Vercel, with Codex assistance. We had previously explored an older application with the same idea; its source and Git history are not this build.” FRIEND: “We tested a real photo submission, task progress, reload persistence, and visitor isolation. Next we would interview local organizers and add deliberate, permission-based collaboration. This version has no automated dispatch or photo AI.” Return to **Overview**.

Before submission, rehearse at normal speaking pace and verify the video stays between 2 and 6 minutes. Consult the organizers if provenance or reuse of existing service infrastructure needs clarification; timestamps alone do not establish eligibility.
